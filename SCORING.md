# The pre-earnings score

The score is 100 points, built from five factors. It is computed on the days *before* a company reports, so every input is public information that existed before the print.

| # | Factor | Points | What it measures |
|---|--------|--------|------------------|
| 1 | Analyst coverage | 25 | One point per analyst covering the name, capped at 25. More coverage means a more reliable consensus to trade against. |
| 2 | Buy-side conviction | 25 | The share of analysts rated buy or strong-buy, scaled to 25 points at 100% bullish. |
| 3 | Strong-buy count | 20 | Two points per strong-buy rating, capped at 20. Conviction is worth more than breadth. |
| 4 | 5-day implied move | 15 | The options-implied move over the next five days. A bigger expected move means a bigger opportunity — and a bigger risk. |
| 5 | Earnings sentiment | 15 | The recent beat/miss record: 15 for a positive trend, 7 for mixed, 0 for negative. |

**Total: 100.**

## Why these five

A pre-earnings setup is not a bet on the quarter's numbers. It is a bet on how much the market has already priced in, how the analyst crowd is positioned, and how much room is left. So the score leans on the two things that can be measured before the print and mean something afterwards:

- **How the crowd is positioned** (factors 1–3) — if every analyst is bullish and there are twenty of them, the consensus is crowded. That is a risk, not a reason to buy, and the score says so.
- **How much the market expects to move** (factor 4) — the implied move is the market's own forecast of the reaction. It sets the size of the prize and the size of the risk.
- **Whether the company has been beating** (factor 5) — the one factor that is about the business rather than about the market.

## How a name becomes the day's pick

A high score alone is not a trade. The day's pick is the strongest-scoring name with real analyst conviction that still has days left before the print — time is the reason to act now rather than later. The runner-up is the second name that clears the same bar.

## What the score is not

It is not a prediction, and it is not investment advice. It is a way of sorting a long calendar down to the handful of names worth reading before they report.
